Frameworks and drivers (storage, HTTP client, system clock, seeded random source). It may import every inner layer. Only `root-provider.tsx` imports the composition root (rule `only-provider-imports-root` of `.dependency-cruiser.cjs`, #411). The role of a class decides its layer: see «The rule of layers 3 and 4» in `docs/architecture/layers.md`. `app/` holds the Expo Router routes, the outer framework layer.

`CompositionRoot` is the composition root (S3-06 in #151, Q9): the one place that knows every piece of every layer and builds it (decision 18 of the class diagram). It builds the drivers `SystemClock`, `SeededRandomSource`, `ConsoleLogger` and `SequentialMatchIds`, the adapters of `src/adapters/` and the game use cases of each game. It builds one `GameEventBus` and gives it to the `ApplyMove` of every game and figure (#362). With the local store of its drivers, it builds one `LocalAchievementCounterRepository`, gives it to `AchievementCounterListener` with the logger of its drivers and subscribes the listener to the bus (#363). The listener writes each failed save of the counters to that logger (#384). A root with no store subscribes no counter listener. Every use case of the root goes through one `decorate(useCase, name)`, which gives it its chain of aspects from the outside in; today the chain holds `LoggingAspect` only (#323). `StartGame` of each game takes one `LevelGameFactories` over the one `BundledLevelRepository` of the root, so it starts a bundled level of Fit or Sort by its id (#172). `game-registry.ts` maps each `GameId` to its variant factory and its screen parts: the state mapper, the cell renderer, the move controls (#175) and the layout of its figure (#170). `registered-games.ts` lists the games, each with the screen parts of its variant (#174 to #177), and gives Fit and Sort their `LevelVariant`. The registry has no default parts: for example, `mergeScreen()` gives Hexa Merge `MergeStateMapper`, `MergeCellRenderer` and `MergeControls`. With the local store of its drivers, the root builds one `LocalProgressRepository` and keeps it for every consumer of the saved progress (#323). It builds `ListAchievements` over the same `BundledLevelRepository`, that repository and the one `LocalAchievementCounterRepository` of the listener (#188, #364), and one `LoadProgress` over the progress repository (#181). Its request names the game, so `loadProgress()` gives that one use case for every game (#410). Over the same two repositories it builds `ListLevels` once, and `listLevels()` gives it to the level selector (#173). Each registered game says with `hasLevels` whether `registered()` took a `LevelVariant`, so the map opens the level selector of Fit and Sort and the game route of Lines and Merge. The store stays optional: a root with no store throws a `RangeError` from `listAchievements()`, `listLevels()` and `loadProgress()`, and `registered-achievements.ts` lists the achievements with the game and the count of each goal. `app/_layout.tsx` only calls the root through `CompositionRootProvider` of `root-provider.tsx`. The provider builds the root once and puts it in the React context as `ScreenServices`, the closed interface of `ui/screen-services.ts` (#411). A hook or a route reads it with `useScreenServices()` and sees no other piece of the root, so the root is no service locator. The root builds the presenter of each screen: `homePresenter()`, `achievementsPresenter()`, `levelSelectorPresenter()` and the `GamePresenter` of `gameSession()`. It knows every piece that a presenter takes, so it is the one place that builds one. Each call builds a new presenter, because a presenter keeps the state of one screen, and the hook of the screen keeps it in React state for the life of that screen. `route-ids.ts` gives the web export the ids of its pages from `registeredGames()` and builds no root (#287, #411).

`device-key-value-store.ts` holds `DeviceKeyValueStore`, the local storage driver (S3-01 in #151, issue #155). It implements `KeyValueStore` of `src/adapters/` with `@react-native-async-storage/async-storage`, so a value survives a restart of the app. `CompositionRootProvider` builds it and gives it to the root, which builds `LocalProgressRepository` over it for the achievements screen (#188). `jest.setup.js` gives every test of the `app` project of Jest the official mock of the package.

`board/` holds the React Native board components (S3-07 in #151, Q8, issue #165). `BoardView` draws the pairs of a coordinate and a cell view of `GameViewModel` on any figure, with the accessible name of the board. `BoardView` reads the screen layout of the figure, `BoardLayout` of `src/adapters/board/` (#393). `CellRenderer` is the interface of the cell renderer of a variant, and `SpaceCellRenderer` tells a free space from an occupied one (#170), and the cell renderer of each variant draws a free space with it. `FitCellRenderer` draws a space of Hexa Fit: a socket, or a stone in the color of its piece (#175). `MergeCellRenderer` draws a Merge tile as a stone in the tone of its value with its number (#177). `BoardView` hands a press on a space to the move controls of the game (#175).
`ui/` holds the parts that the screens share (#169): the design tokens of the team prototype in `theme.ts`, the scene of sky and grass, the hex stone, the wood sign and the lip button, `useHomePresenter()`, which asks the root for `HomePresenter`, and `useSavedProgresses()`, which loads the saved progress of each registered game of a figure through the one `loadProgress()` of the root and gives the pairs to `useHomePresenter()`, so the map shows the record of each game that keeps a high score (#181). `GameStage` is the view of the game screen: it keeps `GamePresenter`, starts the game and shows the board, the score and the outcome. `useGameSession()` keeps the session of one game that the root builds: its presenter over the use cases of the game and its screen parts (#170, #411). `GamePresenter` calls `StartGame`, `ApplyMove`, `UndoMove` and `RedoMove` through `UseCase` (#178). `MoveControls` is the strategy of the move controls of one game: the stage gives it the board and `play()`, which calls `GamePresenter.onMove()`. Each variant has its own controls (#174 to #177): `FitControls` play Hexa Fit with the tray, the rotate button and a press on a space (#175). `MergeControls` play Hexa Merge with one slide button for each way of the figure, and with the prompt that offers to continue when a merge makes the target tile (#177). Since #292 a swipe anywhere on the board plays the way nearest to its angle, and a swipe shorter than 24 px plays nothing. On the web the arrow keys and the keys Q, E, Z and C play the way nearest to the angle of the key: a repeated key plays once, and a key with Ctrl, Meta or Alt plays nothing. `slide-input.ts` holds `swipedWay()` and `useSlideKeys()`, and only this layer reads the platform. The swipe area holds the board only, so a press on a slide button or on the undo button stays a press, and the ring of buttons stays for the screen reader. `app/_layout.tsx` wraps the app in `GestureHandlerRootView`, which the gesture needs. `useAchievements()` keeps the `AchievementsPresenter` that the root builds and gives the view model of the achievements screen (#188). `useLevelSelector(gameId, figure)` keeps the `LevelSelectorPresenter` that the root builds over `listLevels()` and gives the view model of the level selector, and `useGameLevels(figure)` tells the map which games have levels (#173). `GameStage` takes the optional `LevelId` of the game route and starts the game at that level. Since #179 they also show an undo button below the slide buttons, which takes back the last slide.
